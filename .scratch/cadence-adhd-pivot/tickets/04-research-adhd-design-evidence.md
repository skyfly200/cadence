## Question

What does credible evidence say about body doubling, spoken nudges vs alarms (startle response), transition cues, external working-memory offloading, time blindness, and reward/gamification for ADHD adults, and about AuDHD-specific sensory and demand-avoidance considerations? Separate well-supported claims from folk wisdom.

## Resolution

Findings: branch `research/adhd-design-evidence`, file `research/adhd-design-evidence.md` (each claim graded strong / moderate / weak / none found, with sources).

- **Well supported:** ADHD timing deficits (2022 meta-analysis; adult data thin), working-memory deficit, delay aversion (backs short feedback loops), ADHD sensory atypicality (2025 meta-analysis, questionnaire-based, few adult studies). Skills-based CBT with organising/reminder components has an RCT (Safren, JAMA 2010).
- **Weak or unsupported:** body doubling (surveys only; one 26-person EEG study null; no RCTs). Gamification: no efficacy evidence in adult ADHD apps; "novelty decay" and retention figures come from vendor blogs. "Startle, so speak instead of alarm" is folk wisdom: the one adult study found no startle difference and no spoken-vs-alarm ADHD study exists. Transition-cue evidence is from autistic children and unverified.
- **AuDHD:** sensory controls are well justified. PDA is not a validated diagnosis; autonomy-supportive tone is prudent UX, not evidence-backed.
- **Implication:** body double, voice nudges, transition rituals and visual timers are **hypotheses to test with the author's own use**, not settled science; the spec should build them minimally and measure them (see Success signals).

Caveats: some sources returned 403, so figures marked "[unverified: secondary]" in the file need re-checking; only NICE NG87 was reviewed among clinical guidelines.
