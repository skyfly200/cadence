# Fabulous app teardown (for Cadence ADHD/AuDHD pivot)

Ticket: `.scratch/cadence-adhd-pivot/tickets/14-research-fabulous-app-teardown.md`
Researched: 2026-09-29. Branch: `research/fabulous-app-teardown`.

## Method and limits (read first)

- Sources fetched live from the web on 2026-09-29: Fabulous's own site, help center, blog, App Store story; Google's design case study; Trustpilot; App Store review pages; press and independent reviews; Duke/Retraction Watch coverage.
- Page content was read through a summarising fetch tool, so quotes are as extracted, not checked against a screenshot. Treat quotes from third-party pages as worth re-verifying before publishing them anywhere.
- NOT done: I did not install or use the app, and the Wayback Machine was blocked, so I have **no first-hand or archived copy of the 2015-2017 in-app text**. Early-tone evidence comes from contemporaneous reviews and the company's own 2016-2019 posts. Verbatim early coach-letter text is thin; this is the main gap. Recommend the author screenshot the current onboarding and, if any old APK/backup exists, the original.
- No App Store per-version release-note history for 2015-2020 was retrievable. The change story below is assembled from reviews, press and Fabulous's own pages, and is labelled as inference where it is.
- Founding dates conflict across sources: 2013 (Apple App Store story), about 2014 (Wamda 2016, Designli 2019), incubated at Duke in 2015 (Wamda quoting founders). I use "2013-2015".

## 1. Coaching tone and voice

What the sources support:

- **Narrative companion, not a tracker.** Google Design case study, quoting co-founder Taylor Ling: "The app is a companion, guiding users through a story... We use stylized storybook imagery to reinforce goals." Chapter titles are literary, e.g. "A Fabulous Night: In which [your name] learns how to manufacture a great night's sleep." (https://design.google/library/engagement-is-fabulous-health-app)
- **Short personal letters.** Same source: "Fabulous creates short, personalized letters of encouragement that are easy to read and digest." Designli (2019-05-30): "Immediately upon signing up, users get a special 'letter' that's conversational and sounds personal." A 2016 review calls them "Your Letter No. 1", with weekly letters alongside the weekly progress report (techcrackblog, July 2016).
- **Letters used stories and quotes, not commands.** A January 2018 Girlboss piece describes a letter on morning rituals of famous figures, citing Benjamin Franklin's "What good shall I do this day?" and Hemingway writing at dawn.
- **Plain, small asks with a reason.** Onboarding-era copy captured by The Behavioral Scientist (2023-03-06): "According to behavioral science, plan making makes us more likely to follow through on our habits. Think through when, where, and how you will drink water tomorrow." And "Will you commit to drinking water tomorrow? Gratitude is contagious."
- **The "tiny behaviour" philosophy is stated in the company's own words.** About page: "exercising is the outcome, the goal, but it's not the specific behavior"; "Running is not the behavior we're trying to teach you, it's the goal. We're only trying to get you to put your shoes on and start your power song." CEO Sami Ben Hassine: "Behavioral change is not about the final goal. It's about dividing the big changes you seek into smaller behaviors and getting you to accomplish them one at a time." (Designli 2019)
- **Lightly ceremonial framing.** First-launch flow: "An Unexpected Journey"; onboarding "commitment contract" ("I, Jason, will make the most of tomorrow. I will always remember that I will not live forever...") and a "future self" letter ("I'm calling from 2024 because today is an important day for you... I'm healthy, in great shape, and worry-free."). Both quoted from the 2023 Behavioral Scientist critique, who found them "cheesy".
- **Current marketing voice** is more generic: "Find Your Ultimate Daily Routine and Make it Stick"; "an accountability partner in your pocket"; "The New You Awaits" (thefabulous.co, 2026-09-29).

Not found: a verbatim corpus of early letters. I cannot honestly characterise the "original tone" beyond: short, second-person, story-framed, reason-giving, small-ask. The author's own memory is the best evidence for the finer texture.

## 2. Journey and habit structure, and progression

Company's own definitions (help.thefabulous.co "How does Fabulous work?", current):
- **Routines**: "Morning, afternoon, and evening routines you build habit by habit. Each routine has an alarm and a checklist of habits."
- **Journeys**: "Guided programs (3 to 12 weeks) that teach you to build one habit at a time."
- **Challenges**: "5 to 30 days".
- **Coaching**: "3-minute motivational audio sessions" (Daily, Focus, Nightly) plus Coaching Series.
- **Circles** (community), **Guided Activities** (short exercises, "Make Me Fabulous").

Early structure (2016-2018 reviews): four journeys at launch (feel more energised, lose weight, sleep better, focus); everyone starts with "An Unexpected Journey"; the first habit is drink a glass of water on waking, initially "not even every day - just three days out of the week" (Girlboss 2018); then breakfast, then exercise/dance, in roughly 15-day cycles; new chapters or a letter unlock after completing a challenge; next journey unlocks after the previous. Later journeys are framed as three "mountains": Foundations, Struggle, Mastery (Choosing Therapy, updated 2024-05-10). Habit chaining into routines with a timer per habit (Cinnamon Sunrise, user from 2017-06-17).

Progression logic: one habit at a time, minimal first ask, unlocks gated on completion. Friction reported even then: no weekly/non-daily habits, so a "fish three times a week" user gets broken streaks (Cinnamon Sunrise).

## 3. Onboarding

Current flow per The Behavioral Scientist (2023-03-06, the most detailed source): sleep/energy/time questions, "What single change would improve your life?", social proof screen ("96% of users say Fabulous has changed their lives for the better. Fabulous is 22 million users strong."), share-your-plan prompt, email required, commitment contract, "Enjoy your first week. It's free.", future-self letter, first habit (water) with an implementation-intention prompt, then reminders. The critic's overall view: data requested before value shown; behavioural tactics applied awkwardly; long onboarding filters for already-motivated users.

Google Design (2015-16 redesign): early onboarding was a "modified self-select" where users tick boxes committing to water, breakfast, exercise, giving "control and dedication". Reported effect after the Material redesign: daily downloads 300 to 5,000 (company-reported in a Google-hosted case study).

Reviews of the free tier still complain the first days are only "DRINK WATER" reminders (App Store PL reviews, 2026).

## 4. Science claims: what is and is not supported

| Claim | Where made | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Began at Duke's Center for Advanced Hindsight / Behavioral Economics Lab, led by Dan Ariely | Site, About, Apple's App Store story (cofounded 2013; "incubation lab for startups at Duke"; "with the help of behavioral economics professor Dan Ariely") | **Supported as origin/incubation.** Apple's wording is the most precise: an incubation lab plus advice. The About page has Ariely asking the team to define specific behaviours. Ben Hassine "trained as a behavioural economist at CAH" (Designli). The science page names only the Center; the landing page names Ariely. |
| "A Duke University study states people who used Fabulous were more likely to achieve their goals, stay motivated, and feel better about themselves in two weeks" | In-app onboarding | **Not verified.** I found no published paper, citation, effect size or methodology. The Behavioral Scientist notes the researchers are the founders' own institution and no magnitude is given. |
| "96% have made a positive change in their lives" | Site, onboarding | **Unsupported / self-reported.** No method published; critic suspects filtered in-app surveys. User counts also inconsistent across time (20M, 22M, 30M, 37M). |
| "Science-backed", "evidence-based" | Everywhere | **Generic.** The science page cites no studies or links (fetched 2026-09-29). Underlying ideas (implementation intentions, habit stacking, tiny habits, pre-commitment) are real literatures, but the app's own effect is untested in anything I found. The critic argues implementation-intention evidence is overstated for trivial habits like drinking water. |
| Independent trial of Fabulous | none | **None found.** A PubMed search surfaced unrelated habit-app studies; none I could tie to Fabulous. |

Ariely integrity context (dated, and relevant to any "Duke/Ariely" borrowing):
- 2012 honesty paper (with Gino and others) retracted 2021 over fabricated data.
- 2024: Ariely said Duke's investigation found the data falsified but not that he knowingly used fake data; Duke declined to detail (Wikipedia summarising Business Insider / Chronicle of Higher Education).
- **2002 deadlines/procrastination paper (Ariely and Wertenbroch, Psychological Science) retracted 2026-09-02** after Data Colada (posts 2026-08-31 and 2026-09-02) found signs of tampering or fabrication in Study 2; Ariely acknowledged the data "cannot be trusted" (Retraction Watch 2026-09-03). That paper is about self-imposed deadlines and pre-commitment, the same family of idea as Fabulous's commitment contract. I have no evidence Fabulous relied on that paper; this is thematic proximity only.
- Takeaway: do not lean on "Duke/Ariely" credibility as a trust signal in Cadence copy. Cite concrete, independently replicated mechanisms instead, or say plainly "no trial yet".

## 5. Gamification and reward mechanics

Current (help center): a streak is earned by completing at least one ritual (morning, workday, or evening), shown with an orange calendar day; two rituals gives a silver flame; all three a golden flame; **Streak Freeze** (2 by default, more earned at milestones or returning after a break). Also: challenge/journey unlocks, letters as rewards, "A-Team" friend invites, Circles, mood tracker, journal (Designli 2019, Bustle, Choosing Therapy). The early version leaned on unlock-a-letter and story progress rather than flames (inference: flames/freezes are not described in the 2016-2018 reviews).

Reported problems: a habit ticked by accident cannot be unticked (App Store review 2019-10-18); missed logging counts as not done: "If you don't log in...it assumes you didn't perform" (Trustpilot, Sep 2026); non-daily habits break streaks (Cinnamon Sunrise); a 2020-08-16 App Store review calls a notification "acutely manipulative" and fear-based, unsuitable for anxiety.

## 6. Notifications and reminders

- Design: each routine has an alarm; optional push for tasks at set times (help center, Bustle).
- Praise: "Even just the push notifications make me feel..." (Trustpilot, Sep 2026, 5 stars); a 2017 reviewer "tends to snooze them".
- Complaints: "several other random notifications and emails... cannot be easily disabled without silencing all notifications" (Choosing Therapy, updated 2024-05); "I don't need three motivational messages a day" and "Every time you finish a routine...it loads a motivational picture that you then must click out of"; "two or three messages that I have to swipe away... just in order to use Fabulous" (justuseapp aggregation, undated).
- Onboarding email opt-out is a non-standard checkbox (2023 critique).

## 7. How the app and tone changed (early to now)

Evidence-based sequence, with dates:
1. **2015-2016**: Material Design redesign, story-driven, one habit at a time, letters; Google Material Design Award for "Charming Engagement" (2016); App Store "Best App, Self-Care 2018". Reviews of that era praise beauty and simplicity: "It is very pretty and easy to understand" (Roonby 2017-02-25); "This app is so beautiful and really encourages you to change your habits" (App Store, 2017-12-14).
2. **2017-2019**: paid journeys/premium gates appear (Cinnamon Sunrise notes sleep and eating journeys premium; 2019 update adds community Q&A). Free-trial and billing complaints appear (2019-12-21 review).
3. **2020**: "complicated" and "took long to set up... thought this was an all in one solution" (2020-09-07).
4. **2018-2026**: Fabulous positions as "a family of well-being apps" (2018 blog wording), with sister products Clarify (focus/tasks) and HabitatZero (desktop). Reviews now cite "Too many 'sub' apps, too fragmented" (Trustpilot 4-star, 2026-09-11), a surprise install of "their crappy Clarify app with NO notification" (Trustpilot, 2026-09-17), "10000 POP UPS AND UP SELLS... every animation" (2026-08-31), "cluttered interface and wondering if tapping on something is going to pick my pocket" (2026-09-05), and a Reddit quote "NOT for people with ADHD... noisy and labyrinthine" (Headway review, published 2026-05-13, updated 2026-08-21). The store listing is now 660 MB and advertises "morning routines & ADHD help", Dynamic Island, widgets, PiP (App Store, 2026-09-29).
5. Ratings today: App Store 4.4 (89K reviews); Trustpilot 3.2 (3,968 reviews, 52% five-star, 28% one-star).

Not found: an official redesign announcement, or a version-by-version changelog showing when specific clutter (pop-ups, Circles, animations) was introduced. "Later versions cluttered" is strongly supported as a current user-reported condition and as a direction (family of apps, upsells, more surfaces), but the dating of each addition is inference.

## 8. What worked, what hurt

Worked (supported):
- Tiny first ask with a stated reason; one habit at a time.
- Warm, literate, second-person letters and narrative framing that feel like a companion.
- Visual calm and charm in the early redesign (award and download growth, company-reported).
- Adjustable time-of-day routines; bite-sized coaching (3 minutes).
- ADHD-positive user reports exist: "Perfect for an ADHD brain that forgets its own progress" (Trustpilot, Sep 2026); "small wins" and "manageable actions".

Hurt (supported by reviews above):
- Pop-ups, upsells, animation waits, and multiple sub-apps on the main path.
- Long onboarding and paywall-before-value; data requested first; social-share and gratitude "chains" as growth hacks (2023 critique).
- Streak brittleness (no non-daily habits, no undo, unlogged = failed).
- Unclear navigation ("I never know where I'm supposed to touch", Trustpilot via Headway).
- Unverifiable social proof and science claims that erode trust.
- Billing and cancellation friction, especially bad next to ADHD marketing ("advertise it as an ADHD aid, charge $1 for a 14-day trial, and then silently bill you $39", Trustpilot 1-star, 2026-08-18; one user's account, not verified).

## 9. Transferable to Cadence vs avoid

Transfer (design hypotheses; none validated for ADHD/AuDHD by independent trials I found):
- Voice: short, second-person, story-light letters that explain the "why" in one line; ask for the smallest behaviour, define the behaviour, not the goal.
- One thing at a time on screen; reveal gently instead of showing the full map.
- Forgiveness by default (Fabulous itself added freezes; go further: no streak that can "die", allow non-daily cadence, easy undo, missed = neutral).
- A single, adjustable rhythm of check-ins, with each surface individually mutable.
- Honest transparency line: "Small steps built on known ideas; no trial of this app yet."

Avoid:
- Pageantry per completion (modal celebrations, images that must be dismissed), animation waits, pop-up stacks.
- Sister-app cross-promotion, silent installs, fragmented "family" surfaces.
- Fear-framed or guilt notifications; notification categories you cannot silence separately.
- Upfront data harvest, forced email, viral share/gratitude chains, paywall or trial-billing before demonstrated value (also a trust issue for a neurodivergent audience).
- Precise-sounding but unsourced statistics (96%) and borrowed academic prestige, especially Ariely-linked (section 4).
- Marketing as "ADHD help" without an ADHD-tested interaction model.
